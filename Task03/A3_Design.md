
## A3.1 – Choose the Charts (3 Marks)

### 1. PM2.5 Trend Over a Month
Chart Type: Line Chart

Reason:
A line chart shows how PM2.5 levels change
over time and helps identify pollution trends.

X-axis: Date
Y-axis: Average PM2.5 (µg/m³)

### 2. Comparison of Stations
Chart Type: Bar Chart

Reason:
A bar chart compares average PM2.5 levels
across different air-quality stations.

X-axis: Station Name
Y-axis: Average PM2.5 (µg/m³)

### 3. Temperature vs PM2.5
Chart Type: Scatter Plot

Reason:
A scatter plot shows the relationship
between temperature and PM2.5 readings.

X-axis: Temperature (°C)
Y-axis: PM2.5 (µg/m³)

### 4. Hours with the Worst Air
Chart Type: Bar Chart

Reason:
A bar chart compares average PM2.5 levels
for each hour and identifies the hours
with the highest pollution.

X-axis: Hour (0–23)
Y-axis: Average PM2.5 (µg/m³)

### Conclusion
These charts help the pollution control board
identify trends, compare stations, study
relationships and find peak pollution hours.

## A3.2 – Design the Summary Table (2 Marks)

### Station-wise Air Quality Summary

The summary table contains the following columns:

1. Station Name
2. Total Readings
3. Average PM2.5
4. Maximum PM2.5
5. Minimum PM2.5
6. Average PM10
7. Average NO2
8. Average Temperature

### Example Summary Table

| Station | Readings | Avg PM2.5 | Max PM2.5 | Min PM2.5 |
|---------|----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Adyar   | 100      | 45.5      | 90        | 15        |
| Guindy  | 100      | 52.3      | 110       | 20        |
| T Nagar | 100      | 60.2      | 125       | 25        |

Note: The values above are illustrative sample data.

### Purpose

1. Compare pollution levels across stations.
2. Identify stations with high PM2.5 levels.
3. Find maximum and minimum pollution readings.
4. Support air-quality monitoring and reporting.

### Conclusion

The summary table provides a clear overview
of air-quality conditions at different stations.
